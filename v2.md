# PacePay

**HackUTD 2026 · V2 product plan**  
**Working product name:** PacePay  
**Sponsor tracks:** PNC, NVIDIA (Nemotron), Photon  
**Deadlines:** Devpost registration Sat 11:00 PM · final submission Sun 12:00 PM · expo Sun 1:00–4:00 PM

## The idea

**PacePay is a money assistant you save as a contact. It sees your whole financial picture, texts you before money gets tight, and, with your OK, calls whoever you owe to fix it.**

It works in three places, all on the same data and the same calculator:

- **A web dashboard** for seeing everything: every account, health scores, a 60-day forecast, and what-if questions.
- **iMessage** for when something needs you: PacePay texts first, gives you options, and waits for your yes.
- **Phone calls** for acting: PacePay calls a lender or biller, negotiates within your limits, and checks every offer against your forecast before agreeing.

Most PNC teams will build a dashboard and a chatbot. PacePay has the dashboard plus an agent that acts on what it finds.

**How the models split the work:** Nemotron decides and acts. GPT realtime is the voice on calls. Claude double-checks. Code does all the math.

## What it's like to use

Yoshua has eight accounts: checking, savings, a CD, a Roth IRA, a car loan, a personal loan, a credit card, and a mortgage. Yoshua connects them once and sets three rules: keep at least $100 in checking, keep at least $1,500 in savings, and pay no more than $25 in fees.

1. **Saturday: the car needs brakes.** The shop quotes $650. Yoshua texts PacePay a photo of the quote, with no question attached.
2. **PacePay answers right away:** "Got it: $650 to Northside Auto Service, due today. Checking your week." About 15 seconds later it sends a chart of the week and this text:
   > Heads up: −$300 on Tue, Nov 10 if you pay the repair today (mortgage Mon, car payment Tue, paycheck Thu).
   > 1. I ask your lender to move the car payment to Fri. My pick: no new debt
   > 2. Put it on your card: about $14/mo interest
   > 3. Use $400 of savings: drops below your $1,500 floor
   > Reply 1, 2 or 3.
3. **Yoshua replies "1."** PacePay pulls the loan's terms and plans the call. Then it asks for an OK on its limits: "I'll ask to move it to Fri, Nov 13, with $25 max in fees. OK?" Yoshua replies "OK." PacePay gets a second opinion on the plan, texts "Calling your lender now," and calls.
4. **On the call,** PacePay says it's an AI assistant calling for Yoshua with Yoshua's permission, and that the call is transcribed. The lender offers Wednesday. PacePay reads the offer back, checks it, and because Wednesday is still before payday, politely asks for Friday instead. It never mentions Yoshua's balances. The lender agrees to Friday for a $20 fee.
5. **Mid-call, Yoshua's phone buzzes:** "They'll move it to Fri, Nov 13 for $20. You stay above $100. Approve? YES or NO" Yoshua replies "yes," and PacePay confirms on the call.
6. **After the call,** the thread has a summary ("Done. Car payment is now Fri, Nov 13 ($20). Ref A1B2C3.") and the forecast turns green. Yoshua can text "what did they say?" or call PacePay and ask.
7. **The night before,** Thu, Nov 12 at 8:00 PM: "Reminder: your $400 car payment and the $20 fee come out tomorrow. Checking will still have $1,680 after."
8. **Later that month, unprompted,** on Wed, Nov 18:
   > Your $5,000 CD matures Fri, Nov 20.
   > 1. Pay off your card ($2,214) and put $2,786 in savings. My pick: saves about $550 a year, and your emergency fund goes from 0.6 to 1.5 months
   > 2. Renew it: earns about $205 a year
   > Reply 1 or 2 and I'll send the steps.

   Yoshua can compare both in the what-if panel. If Yoshua replies "1," PacePay answers "Got it. I'll text you the steps Friday morning, when the CD matures."

PacePay never moves money or agrees to anything final without Yoshua's yes. He OKs the limits before every call and the deal during it.

## The dashboard

*How each piece looks and behaves is in [UI.md](UI.md). The UI shows each health score with its goal ("Goal: under 30%"), or "On track" once it's met, rather than the green/yellow/red below, which stay as the engine's thresholds.*

1. **All accounts in one place,** grouped the way PNC lists them (checking, savings, CDs and IRAs, loans, credit cards, mortgage), with net worth.
2. **Four health scores,** each with a color, a one-line reason, and what would improve it:

   | Score | What it measures | Green | Yellow | Red |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Cash cushion | Lowest checking balance in the next 30 days | Never below your floor | Below your floor | Below $0 |
   | Emergency fund | Savings ÷ monthly essentials | 3+ months | 1–3 months | Under 1 month |
   | Credit use | Card balances ÷ card limits | Under 30% | 30–50% | Over 50% |
   | Debt-to-income | Monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income | Under 36% | 36–43% | Over 43% |

3. **60-day forecast:** checking balance by day, every bill and paycheck marked, your floor drawn as a line, red wherever the balance dips below it. Click any dip to see the bills that cause it.
4. **What-if panel:** pay a bill now, move a due date, move money from savings, put something on the card, paycheck arrives late, use the CD to pay down the card. The forecast and scores update instantly, side by side with the current plan. Yoshua can also ask what-ifs by text.
5. **Coach chat** (Messages tab): the same conversation as iMessage, mirrored, with an input to message PacePay.
6. **Agent activity** (Activity tab): a live feed of PacePay's plan as a checklist, each tool call labeled with the model that made it, the live call transcript, and every offer checked with pass or fail and why.
7. **Your rules** (Accounts tab): checking floor, savings floor, fee limit, off-limits accounts (the IRA by default), quiet hours.
8. **Negotiation arena** (Arena tab): results from 50 simulated lenders negotiating with PacePay, graded.
9. **Demo controls** (the header): Presenter, Reset, and a clock with a "Skip to Wed, Nov 18" button that jumps to day 11.

## Models and APIs

Nemotron makes every decision in the workflow, because that's what NVIDIA's judges score. Stronger outside models do the jobs they're clearly best at, either as tools Nemotron calls or as checks on Nemotron's work. None of them decides anything.

| Job | Model or API | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Planner: writes the step plan for each event, plans calls, writes the CD advice | Nemotron 3 Ultra through build.nvidia.com (key set up Nov 7; Super is the fallback) | The hardest reasoning in the flow gets NVIDIA's biggest model |
| Orchestrator: carries out the plan, ranks the options (the engine returns them unranked) and calls tools | Nemotron 3 Super | NVIDIA's agentic model, fast enough to keep the loop snappy |
| Call decisions: every response to an offer | Nemotron 3 Super | Made a correct tool call in 0.8 s through TrueFoundry. Nano skipped the tool in testing, so it's a later swap |
| Call voice: hearing, speaking, turn-taking | OpenAI GPT realtime (`gpt-realtime-2.1`, which spoke on a real test call on Nov 7) through LiveKit on the Photon number | The most natural phone voice, and the call stays on Photon, so it qualifies for Best Voice Agent |
| Second opinion before every call | Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`) | A different model family checks the call plan against Yoshua's rules before PacePay dials. Mid-call approvals skip it so the lender isn't kept waiting. |
| Bill photos | Nemotron Nano 2 VL, cross-checked by Claude Opus 5.5 | Read a phone number off a screenshot correctly in 0.9 s. PacePay asks Yoshua outright only if the two models disagree on the amount or due date, or confidence is under 0.8. Otherwise the ack restates the bill, and any reply that isn't a correction confirms it |
| Terms lookup | Embeddings through TrueFoundry (NVIDIA's embedding model if an NVIDIA key is added) | The agent decides when it needs a loan's fine print |
| Simulated lenders | Nemotron 3 Ultra, or Super | NVIDIA lists simulation and eval as a winning pattern |
| Grading the arena | Claude Opus 5.5 | Nemotron shouldn't grade Nemotron |
| Test bill photos | GPT Image 2 through TrueFoundry (Gemini 3 Pro Image as a second option) | 30 realistic bill photos with known amounts and dates to measure the bill reader |
| Charts in iMessage | Rendered from engine data, never AI-generated | Numbers in a picture have to be exact |
| Devpost video opener | Veo 3.1 or Sora 2 | An 8-second opener; everything after it is the real product |
| Messaging | Photon 2.0 iMessage line | Required for Photon |
| Bank data | Seeded household; Plaid Sandbox for a real "connect your bank" step | Plaid is a stretch goal |

Rules for the split:

- **On lender calls, the voice model never sees balances or limits.** It knows Yoshua's name, the loan reference, and the line Nemotron hands it. For every offer, it calls Nemotron's `decide` tool and says what comes back, with a short filler ("one second, let me check that date") while it waits. When Yoshua calls in and passes the PIN, the voice model can fetch numbers through a tool.
- **Measure before swapping.** Run the arena with Nemotron in every seat first. Move a seat to a stronger model only if Nemotron measurably fails there, and say so on the architecture slide.
- **No AI art in the product.** Generated images and video are only for testing and the Devpost video.
- **Stretch goal on the NVIDIA side:** fine-tune Nemotron 3 Nano on negotiation transcripts written by Nemotron 3 Ultra, on a Brev GPU. Don't train on Claude or GPT transcripts, because their terms bar using outputs to train competing models.

**One key for almost everything.** Every model call except the call voice goes through Ram's TrueFoundry gateway: Nemotron Super, Nano and Nano 2 VL (through AWS Bedrock), Claude Opus 5.5, GPT Image 2 and ElevenLabs. GPT realtime isn't on the gateway, so the call voice needs an OpenAI key. Nemotron 3 Ultra runs through build.nvidia.com on the team's NVIDIA key, which is in the shared `.env`. Without the gateway, every Nemotron role can run there too (NVIDIA mode, SPEC §5.4).

## How it works

Everything is TypeScript, so the calculator is one package that every piece shares.

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| Forecast engine | All the math: daily balances, health scores, what-ifs, `check_offer`. Deterministic and unit-tested. It also runs in the browser, so the what-if panel updates instantly. No model ever calculates a number. Models only read numbers in, from a photo or a call, and PacePay confirms each one. |
| Agent service (Node) | For each event, the planner (Ultra, or Super) writes a step plan and Super carries it out with tools. Code enforces the hard gates: no call without Yoshua's pick and Yoshua's OK on the limits, no deal without Yoshua's yes, and every offer goes through `check_offer`. Streams every step to the dashboard. |
| Watcher | Fires on a bill photo and on a daily forecast check (which produces the CD alert). It runs on a demo clock that can jump ahead. |
| Photon bridge | `spectrum-ts` on the iMessage line: proactive texts, numbered options, approvals, free-text questions, chart images, bill photos. |
| Voice worker | LiveKit Agents on the Photon SMS number over SIP, with GPT realtime as the voice and Nemotron's `decide` as its only way to respond to an offer. Handles calls in and out, and hands off to text negotiation if a call fails. |
| Dashboard | Next.js, shadcn/ui and visx, with live updates over server-sent events. Look and behavior: `UI.md`. |
| Data | Postgres with the seeded household, plus loan agreements and card terms as text for the terms lookup. |
| Tracing | Langfuse records every model and tool call for debugging (optional). |

Tools the agents can call: `get_accounts`, `get_forecast`, `simulate`, `check_offer`, `get_terms`, `read_bill`, `second_opinion`, `send_message`, `send_chart`, `request_approval`, `place_call`, `decide`, `post_call_summary`, `schedule_followup`.

PacePay has two numbers on one contact card: the iMessage line for texts and an SMS number for calls, because Photon runs voice on SMS numbers. Run the agent service and voice worker on one always-on machine (a Brev instance works). The dashboard can go on Vercel.

### Trust rules

- PacePay opens every call by saying it's an AI assistant calling for Yoshua with Yoshua's permission, and that the call is transcribed.
- On lender calls it shares only Yoshua's name and the account reference. Never balances, never Yoshua's limits, never ID numbers.
- PacePay reads every offer back before checking it ("So that's Friday, with a $20 fee?"), so a misheard number can't slip through.
- Nothing is final without Yoshua's yes: Yoshua OKs the limits before a call and the deal during it. Every action is logged on the Activity tab.
- If Yoshua doesn't answer a mid-call approval within 60 seconds, PacePay asks the lender to hold the offer, gets a reference number, ends the call, and texts Yoshua.
- If the lender needs to verify identity, PacePay gets a reference number and texts Yoshua to call back. (Stretch: patch Yoshua into the call.)
- Calls in get financial information only from Yoshua's number and only after Yoshua enters a PIN.
- Offers are judged by the engine, not by a model. A payment on payday counts as risky, because the deposit might land after the payment.
- In a negotiation, PacePay asks for the best terms first (a waived fee), concedes only within Yoshua's limits, and never reveals those limits or its reasons.
- Claims to avoid in the pitch: "guarantees no overdrafts," "lenders always agree," "replaces a financial advisor."

## Demo household

Day 0 is Saturday, and every other day is counted from it. Bills come out of checking, and everyday spending (groceries, gas) goes on the credit card: about $710 a month, modeled as $164 posted each Saturday starting day 7. Every bill and paycheck lands on a weekday, because bank transfers don't post on weekends.

| Account | Balance | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Checking | $1,950 | Paycheck $2,000 every other Thursday (next one: day 5) |
| Savings | $1,800 | Emergency fund; Yoshua's floor is $1,500 |
| CD | $5,000 | 12-month, 4.1% APY, matures day 13 (a Friday) |
| Roth IRA | $14,200 | Off-limits |
| Auto loan | $13,400 | $400/month, 6.9% APR, autopay Tuesday (day 3) |
| Personal loan | $3,100 | $150/month, 11.9% APR, due day 23 |
| Credit card | $2,800 of $5,000 | 24.99% APR, $85 minimum, $750 autopay on day 12 |
| Mortgage | $128,000 | $1,200/month including taxes and insurance, autopay Monday (day 2); home value $185,000 |

Other bills from checking: utilities $220 (day 9), phone and internet $120 (day 16), car insurance $180 (day 25). Gross income is $62,000 a year, monthly essentials are $3,065 ($2,355 in bills and minimum payments plus $710 of card spending), and net worth is $60,650.

**Checking this week** (every number checked by script):

| Scenario | Sat | Sun | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Lowest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before the quote | $1,950 | $1,950 | $750 | $350 | $350 | $2,350 | $2,350 | $350 |
| Pay $650 from checking, no fix | $1,300 | $1,300 | $100 | **−$300** | −$300 | $1,700 | $1,700 | −$300 Tue |
| Car payment moved to Wednesday | $1,300 | $1,300 | $100 | $100 | **−$300** | $1,700 | $1,700 | −$300 Wed |
| Car payment moved to Friday, $20 fee | $1,300 | $1,300 | $100 | $100 | $100 | $2,100 | $1,680 | $100 ✓ |

- **Thursday also fails.** It's payday, and banks differ on whether a payment posts before that morning's deposit. PacePay treats payday as risky by rule and asks for Friday.
- **A $35 fee is affordable but not allowed.** Yoshua stays above $100 ($1,665 on Friday), but $35 is over the $25 limit, so PacePay asks for a waiver or a lower fee.
- **The card option** costs about $14/month in interest ($13.54) and takes card use from 56% to 69%.
- **The savings option** drops savings to $1,400, below the $1,500 floor, and takes the emergency fund from 0.6 to 0.5 months.
- **The CD alert** uses the card balance on day 13, not day 0: $2,800 + $164 of spending on day 7 − the $750 autopay on day 12 = $2,214. Paying it off saves about $550 a year (24.99% of $2,214 = $553), and the other $2,786 brings savings to $4,586, or 1.5 months of essentials.

**Health scores on day 0:** cash cushion red (once Yoshua confirms the $650 quote, the forecast counts it as paid from checking today, so Tuesday hits −$300; before the quote, the low is $350), emergency fund red (0.6 months), credit use red (56%), debt-to-income green (35.5%). After the call, the cash cushion turns green. After the CD move, the emergency fund turns yellow (1.5 months) and credit use turns green (about 14%, one month of spending).

## Demo script (3 minutes)

**Setup:** the laptop shows the dashboard, "Yoshua's phone" shows the iMessage thread, and a second team phone is the lender's line. PacePay's contact card, with both numbers, is on screen.

**One demo, three openings.** Choose the first 20 seconds by who is judging:

- **PNC:** start on the dashboard. "Yoshua's money is in eight places. Here's all of it, four health scores, and the next 60 days."
- **NVIDIA:** start on the Activity tab. "Yoshua sent a photo with no question. Nemotron worked out what it means, what it breaks, and what to do, then wrote its own plan and carried it out."
- **Photon:** start on the phone. "PacePay lives in Yoshua's contacts. Watch it text first, then make a call."

**Then:**

1. Text the photo of the $650 quote. PacePay answers right away, then sends a chart of the week and the three options. The dashboard forecast shows −$300 on Tuesday in red.
2. Reply "1." PacePay pulls the terms and plans the call, then texts the limits. Reply "OK." The Activity tab shows the terms lookup, the plan, the second opinion, and the call starting. The lender phone rings. Hand it to the judge: "You're Yoshua's auto lender. Change the deal however you want."
3. The judge negotiates. Whatever they do, PacePay has a path:
   - **Wednesday:** PacePay reads it back, the chart turns red on Wednesday while they're still talking, and PacePay asks for Friday.
   - **Thursday:** PacePay asks for Friday instead, without saying why.
   - **A big fee:** PacePay asks to waive it, then counters at or under $25.
   - **"Early next week":** PacePay asks which day.
   - **"I need to verify the account holder":** PacePay gets a reference number and texts Yoshua to call back.
   - **A flat no:** PacePay thanks them, ends the call, and texts Yoshua the next-best options.
   - **A deal:** Yoshua's phone buzzes mid-call. Reply "yes," and PacePay confirms on the call.
4. Show the summary in the thread, the green forecast, and the cash cushion score turning green.
5. Right after the Done text, PacePay speaks up on its own: **"This works as long as your paycheck lands Thursday. I'll check Thursday night and text you if it hasn't. Reply NO to skip."** The engine saw that Friday only works because Thursday's paycheck lands first (late, checking hits −$320 on Fri). Any okay keeps the watch on; "no" turns it off.
6. Yoshua texts **"My paycheck is going to be late."** PacePay offers the options again, with the call to the lender first. **The demo ends here.** There is no second call.

**Close for the judge in front of you:**

- **PNC:** press "Skip to Wed, Nov 18" in the header (day 11). The CD alert arrives on its own. Compare renewing with paying off the card in the what-if panel.
- **NVIDIA:** show the Arena tab, with the results from the 50 lenders run before the expo, then run three live.
- **Photon:** show the paycheck-late texts in the thread: PacePay keeps context across messages, answers a what-if, and offers the call again with Yoshua's pick still needed.

**If something breaks:** if the call drops or the room is too loud, the judge negotiates with the same negotiator by text. Skip any beat whose Should item isn't ready; the Must-only path is photo → options → limits OK → call → approval → summary → CD alert. Have the judge hold the phone to their ear, not on speaker. ## What v2 must prove

1. A bill photo becomes a bill Yoshua can check: the ack restates the merchant, amount and due date, and a correction ("it's $700") re-runs the case. PacePay asks outright only when the two models disagree or confidence is under 0.8.
2. The forecast shows −$300 on Tuesday and points to the mortgage and car payment as the cause.
3. The options text puts PacePay's pick first and gets every trade-off right.
4. A Wednesday offer fails for the right reason, and PacePay pushes back without revealing balances.
5. A $35 fee is affordable but over Yoshua's limit, and PacePay pushes back without revealing the limit.
6. A vague reply gets a clarifying question.
7. A refusal gets an honest "no deal" and the next-best options.
8. A payday offer is flagged as risky.
9. Every offer heard on a call is read back before it's checked.
10. Nothing is final without Yoshua's two approvals, the limits before a call and the deal, including mid-call. An unanswered mid-call approval ends the call cleanly with a reference number.
11. On lender calls, the voice model never says a balance or limit, because it never has them.
12. The call summary lands in the thread, and "what did they say?" gets a correct answer by text and by phone.
13. Calls in get no financial information unless they come from Yoshua's number and pass the PIN.
14. The CD alert arrives without any prompt from Yoshua.
15. The arena shows 0 limit violations and 0 disclosures across 50 simulated lenders.
16. Someone who sees the demo once can explain what happened and why.
